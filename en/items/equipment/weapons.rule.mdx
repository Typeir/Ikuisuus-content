# Weapons in Damocles

Every weapon in this catalogue is a **pattern**: a name for one way of putting a [base](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-arts#chassis) and a set of [attributes](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-properties) together. Read the row and swing it. The rules for building an object the catalogue does not list are on the [arts](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-arts) and [attributes](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-properties) pages.

Each row lists the pattern, the tier you need in its art to wield it as written, the die a Mundane example rolls, its attributes, its range where it has one, its two masteries, the Masterful Blow its art carries, its base as chassis, finish and [art](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-arts), and its burden. The tier is set by the attribute count: none or one is Familiar, two Proficient, three Expert, four Savant, and Loading (N) refunds slots. The masteries come from the art: at Expert you apply one of them when you hit, at Savant both. Every attribute on the row must belong to an art you hold, and your tier in the weapon's own art caps how many of them the weapon may carry in your hands; a weapon your arts do not cover is an improvised weapon.

## On magical weaponry and Heirlooms

A magical weapon or heirloom is built on a pattern, but its masteries and its Masterful Blow are its own: an heirloom may carry a mastery no art on its chassis provides, or a blow of its own making. Always refer to the item's description.

## Improvised Weapons

If you use an object — such as a table leg, frying pan, or bottle — as a makeshift weapon, see Improvised Weapons in the rules glossary. Also see those rules if you wield a weapon in an unusual way, such as using a Ranged weapon to make a melee attack, or a weapon your arts do not cover.

### Improvised Weapons (Rules Glossary)

- An improvised weapon is an object wielded as a makeshift weapon, such as broken glass, a table leg, or a frying pan. A catalogue weapon also counts as an improvised weapon if it's wielded in a way contrary to its design, or if your arts do not cover it; if you use a Ranged weapon to make a melee attack or throw a Melee weapon that lacks the Thrown attribute, the weapon counts as an improvised weapon. An improvised weapon follows the rules below.
- **Proficiency**: Don't add your Tier Bonus to attack rolls with an improvised weapon.
- **Damage**: On a hit, the weapon deals [% 1d4 damage %] of a type the Game Director thinks is appropriate for the object.
- **Range**: If you throw the weapon, it has a normal range of [= 4 stride =] and a long range of [= 12 stride =].
- **Weapon**: Equivalents. If an improvised weapon resembles a catalogue weapon, the Game Director may say it functions as that weapon and uses that weapon's rules. For example, the Game Director could treat a table leg as a Club.
