# Attack Alterations

An attack alteration is a **special melee attack** that replaces one of your attacks when you take the **Attack action**. Rather than dealing weapon damage, it **imposes a condition**, **forces movement**, or **establishes physical control** over a target.

Every creature has access to the core alterations below. Certain vocation features, feats, and abilities introduce additional attack alterations with their own rules.

---

## Using an Attack Alteration

When you take the **Attack action**, you may substitute **one attack** for an attack alteration.

- If you can make multiple attacks with the Attack action, determine how many attacks can be replaced by alterations based on **the specific alteration's rules**.

_For example, if an alteration can only replace one attack, you can use it once per Attack action, even if you have multiple attacks._

---

## Grapple

You seize a creature and hold it in place. You must have **at least one free hand**.

You can grapple creatures of varying sizes depending on your strength and your size, as described in the following table:

| Your Strength | Maximum Target Size |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| 0-10          | Your size           |
| 11-15         | One size larger     |
| 16-20         | Two sizes larger    |
| 21+           | Three sizes larger  |

In order for you to grapple a creature, you must first **land an attack** against it. You can use your unarmed strike or a weapon attack with a weapon with the [bind](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-properties) property to initiate the grapple, but you must **declare** that you are using the attack to grapple **before making the attack roll**.

If the attack hits, you can attempt to grapple the target as part of the same action.

**Contest:** Strength (Athletics) vs. the target's Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics).

**On a success:** The target gains the [# kw:condition:grappled #] condition — its speed becomes 0 and it cannot benefit from any bonus to its speed.

You can release the grapple at any time.

### Escaping a Grapple

A [# kw:condition:grappled #] creature can take a Major Action to escape. It makes a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check contested by the grappler's Strength (Athletics). On a success, the [# kw:condition:grappled #] condition ends.

### Moving a Grappled Creature

When you move, you can drag or carry the [# kw:condition:grappled #] creature with you. Your speed is **halved** unless the creature is two or more sizes smaller than you.

## Shove

You push a creature off balance, either to knock it [# kw:condition:prone #] or to create distance.

**Contest:** Strength (Athletics) vs. the target's Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics).

**On a success:** Choose one:

- Knock the target [# kw:condition:prone #], or
- Push the target **[= 1 stride =]** straight away from you.

---

## Restrain

You press your advantage on a [# kw:condition:grappled #] creature to pin it entirely. You can only attempt to Restrain a creature that is already [# kw:condition:grappled #] by you.

**Contest:** Strength (Athletics) vs. the target's Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics).

**On a success:** The target gains the [# kw:condition:restrained #] condition in addition to [# kw:condition:grappled #] — its speed is 0, attack rolls against it have advantage, and its own attack rolls and Dexterity saving throws have disadvantage.

Maintaining a Restrain counts as using one hand. If you lose the free hand used to maintain it (by being forced to drop the hold, losing the limb, or taking the [# kw:condition:incapacitated #] condition), the [# kw:condition:restrained #] condition ends, though the [# kw:condition:grappled #] condition may remain depending on circumstances.

### Escaping a Restrain

A [# kw:condition:restrained #] creature can spend one Major Action to escape. It makes a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check contested by the grappler's Strength (Athletics). On a success, the [# kw:condition:restrained #] condition ends, but the [# kw:condition:grappled #] condition remains.

## Knockout Blow

You may declare an attack you make as if it was intended to **incapacitate rather than kill**. You roll said attacks with a **-2 accuracy and damage penalty**.

**On a knockout:** If the attack would reduce the target to 0 hit points or less, the creature drops to 0 hit points and is immediately **stable** — it does not make death saving throws and does not accrue negative hit points from the attack.

A creature knocked out in this way is [# kw:condition:unconscious #] until it **regains hit points**, or until [% 1d4 %] hours have passed.

_Ranged attacks and spell attacks cannot be declared knockout blows._

---

## Masterful Blow

A Masterful Blow is a **special strike technique** tied to a specific weapon type. Rather than replacing an attack, it **augments a hit** — when you land a weapon attack, you may spend a use to apply the weapon's Masterful Blow effect.

Full rules, save DCs, use limits, and the complete table of Masterful Blow effects are in [Weapon Properties](/en/library/rules/arms-armour-and-burden/weapon-properties#masterful-blows).

## Feature-Based Attack Alterations

Some vocation features, feats, and abilities introduce their own attack replacements or alterations. These function like the core alterations above — they **replace one attack** in the Attack action — but may have different **checks**, **conditions**, or **target requirements**. Examples include:

- Bladesinger multiattack substitution
- Certain maneuvers that impose conditions on a hit
- Feat-granted special attacks (such as trip, disarm, or redirect)

Each such feature defines its own procedure. Where a feature's rules conflict with the general constraints above, **the feature's specific rules take precedence**.
