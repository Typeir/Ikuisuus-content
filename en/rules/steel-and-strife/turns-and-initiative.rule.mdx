# Turns and Initiative

Initiative determines the order of turns during a combat encounter. One round represents about 6 seconds of game time. When combat starts, every participant rolls initiative; the result fixes the order for the encounter unless an effect changes it.

---

## The Order of Combat

1. **Determine surprise.** The Game Director determines whether anyone involved is surprised.
2. **Establish positions.** The Game Director places all characters and monsters and determines distances.
3. **Roll initiative.** Everyone rolls initiative (a Dexterity check), determining the order of turns.
4. **Take turns.** Each participant acts in initiative order — move up to your speed, take your actions as you see fit.
5. **Begin the next round.** When everyone has had a turn, the round ends. Repeat from step 4 until combat ends.

Ties: among Game Director-controlled creatures, the Game Director decides order. Among tied player characters, the players decide. Between a PC and a monster, highest dexterity goes first, otherwise the Game Director may have each roll a d20 (highest goes first).

The initiative order remains fixed from round to round, unless an effect explicitly changes it.
Creatures can lower their initiative permanently if they choose to, but cannot raise it.

---

## Your Turn

The turn operates under the "Two actions, one reaction" principle. It is encapsulated in the movement; movement happens — Or doesn't — while you act.

- **Movement.** Move up to your speed. You can break movement before and after your actions. You can spend movement to drop [# kw:condition:prone #] or to become [# kw:condition:steady #] (see [Actions in Combat](/en/library/rules/steel-and-strife/actions-in-combat#movement)).
- **Two action points.** You have a pool of two action points to spend as you see fit. You are limited to one Major Action per turn, unless a feature says otherwise.
- **One Major Action.** You can consume one action point from your pool for a Major Action. This action is used for attacking, casting a spell with a casting time of 1 Major Action, or any of the actions listed in [Actions in Combat](/en/library/rules/steel-and-strife/actions-in-combat).
- **One Minor Action.** You can consume one action point from your pool for a Minor Action. Used for quicker tasks — drinking a potion, making an off-hand attack, issuing a command to a companion.
- **You have one Reaction per round.** Used for off-turn responses. You regain your Reaction at the start of your turn.

You can forgo any or all of these whenever you see fit.

---

## Major Actions

The Attack action is a Major Action. So are Dodge, Help, Hide, Cast (For Major Spells), Ready, Dash, Disengage, Search, and Use. These are Actions that require focus and attention.

---

## Minor Actions

A Minor Action handles tasks too **quick** to demand the focus of a Major Action. Drinking a potion, issuing a short command, following the arching motion of a dominant swing with the off-hand — these are Minor Actions.

---

## Reactions

A reaction is an instant response to a trigger, taken on your turn or another creature's turn.

- You have **one reaction per round**. Some features may add, remove, or lift restrictions on actions and reactions.
- If a reaction interrupts another creature's turn, that creature resumes its turn after the reaction resolves.
- The **opportunity attack** is the most common reaction — see [Making an Attack](/en/library/rules/steel-and-strife/making-an-attack#opportunity-attacks).
- The **Ready** action lets you set a trigger and hold a response — see [Actions in Combat](/en/library/rules/steel-and-strife/actions-in-combat).

---

## Reflexes

A **reflex** is a response sonething asks of you. Nothing is spent to make one, and no limit governs how many you make or decline in a round.

- A **saving throw** is a reflex. So is any **check imposed on you**, rather than one you chose to attempt.
- You can **choose** whether to act upon a **reflex** or **not**, if you choose not to, you **suffer the consequences** of **failing** its triggering **effect**, if any.
- A reflex costs **nothing**, and can be attempted regardless of **conscious state**.
- A creature that is [# kw:condition:unconscious #] or [# kw:condition:dying #] still makes the reflexes its state calls for. **Death saving throws** are reflexes.

---

## Action Degradation

On your turn, you may **convert any of your action points** into an **additional Reaction** for the round. Sacrifice proactive capacity for reactive readiness.
You must declare degradation **during your turn**. You cannot degrade between turns or in response to a trigger.
You may degrade **any number of action points**, and **both Reactions and action points** may be degraded into **Interactions**. Degradation lasts until the start of your next turn.

---

## Interactions

You can interact with one object or environmental feature for free during your movement or action — opening an unlocked door, drawing a weapon as part of an attack, picking up a dropped item. A second interaction costs a Minor Action.

---

## Surprise

- Surprise occurs when one side does not notice a threat that initiates combat.
- If neither side tries to be stealthy, they automatically notice each other.
- Otherwise, the Game Director compares Dexterity (Stealth) checks of anyone hiding against the passive Wisdom (Perception) of creatures on the opposing side.
- A **surprised** creature cannot move or take actions on its first turn, and cannot take a reaction until that turn ends.
- Individual members of a group can be surprised even if others in the group are not.

---

## Initiative Notes

- Initiative is fixed per encounter; effects that alter turn order state how and when they modify it.
- The Game Director may make one initiative roll for an entire group of identical creatures, so they all act on the same count.
- Action Degradation is always voluntary — no rule or condition forces a creature to degrade.
