# Grievous Wounds

Not all harm heals with a night's rest. Grievous Wounds represent deep, lasting injury; cracked ribs, torn sinew, arcane lacerations...

---

## What Are Grievous Wounds?

Grievous Wounds are a **separate numerical pool** tracked alongside hit points. 
They are negative Hit Points: damage that has burrowed past the flesh and into the bone, the self, or the will.

- Grievous Wounds are **stacking** — a creature can accumulate any amount, to a maximum of their maximum HP, at which point they die.
- A creature with Grievous Wounds is not visibly spurting blood; they are _diminished_.

---

## Taking Grievous Wounds

When an effect inflicts Grievous Wounds, the amount is subtracted from **both maximum HP and current HP** (current HP to a minimum of 1).

> **Example:** A creature with 40 maximum HP and 28 current HP takes 12 Grievous Wounds. Its maximum HP drops to 28, and its current HP drops to 16.

Grievous Wounds do not cause unconsciousness on their own — only the HP loss they create can drop a creature to 0.

---

## Recovering Grievous Wounds

The only way to reduce grievous wounds is **spending Hit Dice**, or through highly specific effects; standard healing methods do not work on them.

- During a Rest, a creature may spend Hit Dice to reduce its Grievous Wounds.
- Roll each spent Hit Die as normal, with all the modifiers they would usually incur. The result is subtracted from the Grievous Wound pool.
- These Hit Dice restore no hit points until the wounds are cleared.
- Excess is carried over as healing.

> **Example:** A creature with 18 Grievous Wounds and a CON of 0 spends 2 Hit Dice, rolling a 5 and a 4.
> Grievous Wounds drop to 9. No hit points are restored.
> A creature with 18 Grievous Wounds and a CON of 6 spends 3 hit dice, rolling a 2, a 3, and a 10, for a total of 33.
> The creature clears all of its grievous wounds, and regains 15 hit points.

---

## Lost Hit dice and Broken Hit dice

Some effects will have characters incur on **Lost hit dice**, these effects cause hit dice to be temporarily lost until a certain condition is met, effectively reducing their hit dice count by an amount equal to the lost hit dice count.
If a hit dice is instead **Broken**, the same effects apply but it is entirely **irrecoverable** unless an effect can rewrite the act itself, like the [True Wish](/en/library/spells/true-wish) spell.

## Grievous Wounds and Death

- If Grievous Wounds reduce a creature's maximum HP to 0, the creature dies.
- A creature below 1HP with Grievous Wounds still makes death saving throws normally. The GW pool does not interact with death saves.
- If a creature dies while carrying Grievous Wounds and is revived, the GW pool persists — restarting life does not erase lasting injury.

---

## Sources of Grievous Wounds

Grievous Wounds are inflicted by specific effects, not by ordinary damage:

- **MISDEED overshoot:** Overexerting oneself through DEEDS (see [Deeds, Grit, and Misdeeds](/en/library/rules/steel-and-strife/deeds-grit-and-misdeeds)).
- **Certain Vocation Features:** [Scions](/en/library/character-creation/vocations/scion) fuel their immense capabilities to **Sculpt Magic** with their own bodies, sustaining **Grievous Wounds** in the process.
- **Certain Monster Features:** Abilities that rend soul, shatter bones, or drain vitality.
- **Environmental hazards:** Exposure, corrupted terrain, akashic fallout.
- **Game Director adjudication:** Any effect the Game Director deems too severe for simple HP loss.

Ordinary weapon attacks, spells, and falling do **not** inflict Grievous Wounds unless explicitly stated.
