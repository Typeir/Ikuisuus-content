# Rolling and Special Rolls in Damocles

Not all rolls in Damocles follow the same structure. This page outlines the special roll variants used across the system beyond the standard checks and rolls described in [Checks and Rolls](/en/library/rules/the-measure-of-the-self/checks-and-rolls). For the foundational rules on making ability checks, saving throws, and standard mechanics, see that page. For the full description of abilities and skills, see [Abilities and Skills](/en/library/rules/the-measure-of-the-self/abilities-and-skills).

---

## The Test

A test is the **roll** of a **d20** to determine the plausible outcome of an interaction. 

**Tests** are governed by the following rules:

---

## Standard Tests

Most **Tests** in Damocles follow a **d20 roll + modifiers** system:

- **Ability Checks** – d20 + relevant ability modifiers
- **Attack Rolls** – d20 + relevant attack modifiers
- **Saving Throws** – d20 + relevant save modifiers

---

## The dice states:

A dice roll has a defined set of states through which it is referred to:

- **Nascent**: before it is cast, what is applied unconditionally to it. 
>  Example: **Strength** is applied nascently to a check that involves strength, such as **Athletics**, or an attack with a **Broadsword**.
- **Cast**: while the dice is rolling and the outcome has not settled; the lifetime of the roll itself.
- **Posthumous**: After the dice is cast, rolled, and the result is **known**. Certain effects are applied posthumously.
>  Example: [_Argent strands_](/en/library/spells/argent-strands) is applied to a roll once the result is known.

---

## Test Modifiers

These concepts govern the outcome of the final roll result, modifiers change the way the result is determined:

### Accuracy

Accuracy determines the **sum** of **Modifiers** added to a **Test**.
Accuracy is also added to the **Difficulty Class** _(DC)_, provided the **feature** imposes a **saving throw** or **check** of any kind.
Each individual feature has a different keyed **modifier** for its accuracy and damage.

---

### Critical Roll

Critical rolls occur when the dice roll on a certain face, meeting a threshold or not being able to surpass it.
Critical rolls are scoped to **Ability Checks**, **Attack Rolls** and **Saving Throws**
A critical success is attained when a threshold is met. Certain features change this threshold.
A critical failure is the inverse, and occurs when a certain threshold is not surpassed.
The normal threshold for a critical success is **20**, and the normal threshold for a critical failure is **1**.
For all rolls save for **attacks**, a **critical success** does not mean an automatic success, and a **critical failure** does not mean an automatic failure.
These often carry extra consequences on top of a regular failure or success.

_Some things are bound to happen, no matter what the die says, others are forbidden by the whims of fate_.


---

### Advantage and Disadvantage

When rolling with **Advantage**, roll **twice** and take the **higher result**.  
When rolling with **Disadvantage**, roll **twice** and take the **lower result**.  
These cancel each other out and are **not stackable**.

These follow standard rules.

---

### Deeds and Grit

Deeds are a resource that can be used to [# kw:grit #] through a challenge, bridging the gap to success by changing the odds from the source.
Spending a Deed before a roll lets you declare [# kw:grit #].

See [Deeds, Grit, and Misdeeds](/en/library/rules/steel-and-strife/deeds-grit-and-misdeeds) for the full rules.

The Game Director can reward exceptional roleplay, granting the party **+1 Deed**. 
Declare before the die is cast; the Deed is spent whether you succeed or fail. 

---

### Bonus and Penalty Die

Certain features will grant additional dice to be rolled with the original, altering the result.

- **Bonus Die**: The result is added to the total.
- **Penalty Die**: The result is subtracted from the total.

These die are **cast** along with the original cast.
These die may be present in rolls other than **Attack Rolls** **Ability Checks** and **Saving Throws**.

---

### Flooring

A floored roll has its dice result changed to a specific minimum if it would be under it, a floor of 10 has any result on the dice it floors have a minimum value of 10.
> Example: A [_Pellervi_](/en/library/character-creation/bloodlines/pellervi) with **Dependable** rolls a 3 on a skill check, which fails by just two. 3 is under 5, so it becomes a 5 instead. The pellervi has a high enough modifier to turn that failed roll of 3 into a success with a 5, so they succeed.   


---

## Defying the outcome

Some features let you modify the outcome **posthumously** rather than **nascently**; after the die has been cast and the outcome has been determined.

### Chancing

Unlike Deeds and Grit, **chancing** allows one to roll the entire roll again, and must take the new result.
This includes all of the **Bonus** and **Penalty** dice.
A chanced roll does not carry **advantage** or **disadvantage** from the original roll with it.
A **critical roll** can **never** be **chanced**, regardless of outcome. [_Pellervi Luck_](/en/library/character-creation/bloodlines/pellervi) is the sole exception.

### Swaying

A swayed roll acts exactly like a **Bonus or Penalty** dice, but **Posthumously**.
Once the result is known, you may alter it after the fact, with these dice; Potentially altering the course of fate.

---

## Habitual Rolls

### Attack Rolls

For attack rolls, critical hits are automatic success, and critical misses are automatic failures, regardless of modifiers. A 1 will never hit, and a 20 will never miss.

- A **result of 20** on an **attack roll** is a **critical hit**.  
  **All damage dice are doubled** on a critical hit.
- A **result of 1** on an **attack roll** is a **critical miss**.  

---

### Skill Checks and Saving Throws

- **Skill checks and Saves have Critical**.
- A **result of 1** is **not an automatic failure**.
- A **result of 20** is **not an automatic success**.

Only the **final total matters**. Some tasks are **impossible no matter the roll**, and some are **inevitable no matter the hand**.

---

## Special Roll Types

Certain Roll types are more uncommon or require special consideration

### Chained

A roll may be **Chained**, resolving over several attempts rather than one.

A chain is written **Chained(successes/failures)**:
  - **Chained(3/1)** completes on **three successes** and fails on **one failure**. 
  - **Chained(3/3)** completes or fails on **three** of either.

Successes and failures **need not be consecutive**, and either count may be reached first.

When a chain **completes**, the effect it was resisting **ends**. When a chain **fails**, the entry states what failing it does.

A **Chained** **Resist** is rolled **at the end of the sufferer's turn**, one at a time, per turn.

A common example of a **Chained** effect are **Death Saving Throws**. A creature that is **[# kw:condition:dying #]** rolls **Chained(3/3)**. On **three successes**, it stabilizes. On **three failures**, it dies.

--- 

### Raw Roll

A Raw Roll **[RR]** is a check where:

- You roll **d20 + specified modifier _(score, tier, expertise, familiarity)_, if any**.
- The roll cannot be **Chanced**, **Swayed**, given **Bonus** or **Penalty** dice of any kind, and is not affected by bonuses whose **source** is other than you.
- Grit cannot be used on raw rolls.
- **Alterations to the self** do affect this roll—if one **Grafts** a monstrous appendage that increases their strength tenfold, they may succeed where they otherwise couldn't.

Raw Rolls represent **accumulated comprehension and effort**, **extreme luck**, or **the intrinsic self**, not talent or magic.

---

#### When to Use Raw Rolls

Use a Raw Roll **[RR]** when:

- The task reflects **pure understanding or practice**, not circumstantial luck.
- The character is **learning, crafting, researching**, or otherwise advancing themselves over time.
- The world demands a **test of will or self**, such as handling forbidden artifacts or engaging with metaphysical machinery.

---

### Confirmed Roll

A **Confirmed Roll [CR]** represents actions where success requires **both execution and confirmation**.

- Roll **d20 + relevant modifiers** against the DC.
- If the **first roll succeeds**, **roll again** (using the same modifiers).
- **Both rolls must succeed** for **full success**.
- If only the **first roll succeeds**, a **partial success** **always occurs**.

---

#### Example of Partial Success

For instance, if you **throw a potion at an ally**, the first roll determines if you hit them at all—this may result in the potion **striking their body and dealing damage**.  
However, if the **second roll fails**, the potion **won't activate properly**, and no healing occurs.

_Success is a fickle word._

---

#### When to Use Confirmed Rolls

Use a **Confirmed Roll [CR]** when:

- The action is resisted by **metaphysical forces** (e.g., null-magic zones, artifact activation, curses or blessings).
- The consequences of failure are **catastrophic**, or the action represents a **chain of uncertain steps**.
- The action could **plausibly happen out of sheer luck**, but carries **adverse consequences** for partial failure.
- The Game Director determines the situation involves **compounding risk**, or where multiple stages of failure are meaningful.

---

### Deliberate Roll

A **Deliberate Roll [DR]** allows a character to **default to a fixed result** when no danger, urgency, or metaphysical risk is involved.

If the check is **repeatable and safe**:

- Spend **25 minutes**: Treat the d20 as a **10**.
- Spend **50 minutes**: Treat the d20 as a **20**.

After which, relevant modifiers apply accordingly.

This represents careful, methodical work, study, or focus.  
It does not apply when circumstances prevent deliberate action (combat, life-threatening conditions).

---

#### When to Use Deliberate Rolls

Use a **Deliberate Roll [DR]** when:

- The task is **safe, repeatable, and allows for patient effort**.
- Time is available, and there's no penalty for taking longer.
- The check represents **study, inspection, or careful execution** without pressure.

## Passive Checks

A **passive check** represents the average result for a task done repeatedly, or the result the Game Director uses when they want to determine success without calling for a roll—for example, quietly determining whether characters notice a hidden creature.

**Passive total formula:** 10 + all modifiers that normally apply to the check (ability modifier, tier bonus if applicable, and any situational modifiers).

- If the character has **advantage** on the check, **add 5** to the passive total.
- If the character has **disadvantage** on the check, **subtract 5** from the passive total.

Passive checks are most commonly used for **Wisdom (Perception)**. A character's passive Perception acts as a floor: the Game Director compares it against a hidden creature's Stealth check result instead of calling for an active roll each time.
