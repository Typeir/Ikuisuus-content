# Coherent Stakes

A stake is what changes when the roll resolves. If nothing changes, there were no stakes, and the roll was decoration.

Damocles asks more of its stakes than most systems, because its costs are slow. A wound heals over ten days, not overnight. A curse outlives the session it was contracted in. A spent [# kw:Recovery #] moves the calendar. Consequences at that scale only work if the table knew what was being wagered before the die left the hand.

---

## Declare Before the Roll

Say all three aloud, every time, in one breath:

- **What is being attempted.** The specific action, not the intent.
- **What success gets.** Concrete.
- **What failure costs.** Concrete, and never "nothing".

"Roll Athletics" is not a stake. "Roll Athletics — clear the gap and you reach the far ledge before it collapses; fail and you are on the near side when it does, with the wolves" is.

If you cannot state the cost, you have found out that the roll should not happen. Narrate the outcome and move on. See [Tests, Trials, and Tribulations](/en/library/rules/tales-tests-and-tropes/tests-trials-and-tribulations).

---

## Proportion

A stake must be commensurate with the action that risked it. This is the clause most often broken, and it breaks in both directions.

- **Overscaled.** A failed Perception check kills a character. The table learns that any roll may be fatal, and stops volunteering to act. Caution is not tension.
- **Underscaled.** A failed roll costs an hour of in-game time nobody was tracking. The table learns rolls do not matter, and stops caring which way they land.

Calibrate to the fiction, not to the sheet. A character picking a lock in an empty corridor risks time and a broken pick. The same character picking the same lock during a rite in the Bones of Selkara risks being caught mid-rite. Same DC, same roll, entirely different stake — because the situation, not the difficulty, sets the price.

---

## Stakes Must Be Real

A declared cost that never arrives is worse than no cost, because it teaches the table to discount your declarations. Once you have named the wolves, the wolves must be there on a failure.

This is where coherence and the [contract of mechanics](/en/library/rules/tales-tests-and-tropes/the-contract-of-mechanics) meet: softening an announced consequence is the same breach as fudging a die. If a stake turns out to be more than you wanted to spend, that is information about how you set it, not permission to withdraw it.

The discipline is upstream. Do not name a cost you are unwilling to inflict.

---

## Failing Forward Is Not Failing Free

A failure that stops the story is a design error; a failure that costs nothing is a different design error. The move is to advance the situation while extracting the price:

- The door opens, and the noise brings something.
- The ritual is deciphered, and reading it costs a level of [# kw:condition:exhaustion #].
- The wound is treated, and it will scar — see [Grievous Wounds](/en/library/rules/life-blood-and-ruin/grievous-wounds).

The plot moves. The party is diminished. That is the shape Damocles wants, and it is why the attrition systems exist: they give you somewhere to put a cost that is not the character's life.

---

## Stakes Above the Scene

The same three questions govern arcs. What is the campaign wagering, what does winning it look like, and what does the world lose if the party fails or never arrives?

Answer those and the ten-day rests, the permanent curses, and the deaths stop reading as arbitrary cruelty. They become the price of a thing worth the price — which is the whole argument of [The Eulogy of Damocles](/en/library/rules/tales-tests-and-tropes/the-eulogy-of-damocles). Nothing survives. The stake was never survival. It was whether it mattered that you tried.
