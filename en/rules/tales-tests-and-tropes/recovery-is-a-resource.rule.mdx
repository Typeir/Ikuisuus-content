# Recovery is a Resource

In most games, rest is punctuation. The party clears a dungeon, the Game Director says "you rest", and the sheet resets. Damocles does not work that way. A [# kw:Recovery #] is **ten uninterrupted days of low-exertion living**, and those ten days are spent out of the same account the world draws from. Every full reset costs the party a season of the story.

This is deliberate. Hit points and spell slots are not the scarce resource in Damocles. **Time is.**

---

## What the Cost Actually Buys

Read the ledger honestly before you set a pace:

- A [# kw:Respite #] is thirty minutes to an hour. Cheap. Spend it freely.
- A [# kw:Repose #] is eight hours. Affordable most nights, and the only thing that sheds [# kw:condition:exhaustion #].
- A [# kw:Recovery #] is ten days. Expensive. It restores spell slots, vocation features, hit dice, and the things nothing else touches.

See [Resting and Recovery](/en/library/rules/life-blood-and-ruin/resting-and-recovery) for the mechanics. What concerns you as Game Director is the shape those numbers force on a campaign: parties cannot burn every resource in every fight and expect to be whole tomorrow. They must ration. Rationing is a decision, and decisions are the game.

---

## The World Does Not Wait Ten Days

The first instinct of a new Game Director is to hand out a Recovery whenever the party looks tired. Resist it — not by refusing, but by charging the honest price.

Ten days is long enough for the siege to break. Long enough for the caravan to leave without them. Long enough for the revenant's ritual to finish, for the fever to take the child they promised to return for, for the snow to close the pass until spring. When the party declares a Recovery, do not ask "may I?" — advance the clock and tell them what it cost.

Do this consistently and the table learns the lesson without being lectured: **resting is a move, not a pause.** A party that spends ten days healing has chosen to let something else happen. That choice belongs to them, and so does the consequence.

The corollary binds you too. If the world never moves during a Recovery, you have made the resource free, and the ration economy collapses. See [The World Is a Character](/en/library/rules/tales-tests-and-tropes/the-world-is-a-character).

---

## Interruption Is Not Punishment

A Recovery interrupted by strenuous combat, levelled spellcasting, or intense exertion loses **half its progress, and never less than two days**. Players will read this as a trap. It is not one — it is the pressure that makes the ten days feel like ten days.

Use it plainly:

- Announce the risk before the party commits. "You can hole up here, but this is a Nigredo-touched valley, and things walk it."
- Do not manufacture an interruption because the Recovery felt too easy. If the world was already moving toward them, the interruption is earned. If it was not, inventing one teaches the table that rest is never safe, and a table that believes rest is never safe stops resting — which removes the decision entirely.
- Let an easy encounter pass without cost when nobody fell [# kw:condition:unconscious #] and no recovery-gated resource was spent. The rule already says so. Saying it aloud builds trust.

---

## Downtime Is the Other Half

Ten idle days are wasted days. A Recovery running in parallel with [Downtime, Learning, and Training](/en/library/rules/hearth-and-station/downtime-learning-and-training) turns a cost into an investment: a language learned, a trade practised, a contact cultivated, a debt paid down.

Offer downtime every time a Recovery is declared. It converts the most expensive resource in the game from a tax into a turn, and it gives players who track lifestyle, crafting, and reputation something to do with the calendar you just spent.

---

## In Practice

- Price the Recovery in story, not in gold. What slipped away?
- Never grant one silently. Make the party declare it.
- Move at least one off-screen thread forward every time. One is enough.
- Offer downtime in the same breath.
- When the party refuses a Recovery they clearly need, let them. Attrition is the point.

A campaign where the party is always whole has no attrition, and a campaign without attrition has no [Coherent Stakes](/en/library/rules/tales-tests-and-tropes/coherent-stakes).
