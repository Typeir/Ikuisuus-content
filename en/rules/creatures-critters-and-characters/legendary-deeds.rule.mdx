# Legendary Deeds

Legendary creatures possess an innate capacity for extraordinary actions that set them apart from common foes. **Legendary Deeds** unifies all such abilities under a single resource pool.

---

## Core Mechanics

### Deed Pool

A legendary creature has a number of **legendary deeds per round**, specified in its stat block (typically 1-3). It regains all expended deeds **at the start of its turn**.

> **Example**: A creature with "**3 legendary deeds per round**" can spend up to 3 deeds between its turns, regaining all 3 when its next turn begins.

### Deed Cap

Three limits apply at once, and the tightest one binds.

1. **Per creature**: a creature spends no more than its own deed pool between its turns.
2. **Per turn**: at most one legendary deed resolves in any single turn, across every creature in the encounter.
3. **Per round, globally**: the total number of deeds spent by all legendary creatures in a round is capped by the number of player characters actively engaged in the encounter.

The third limit is what keeps two legendary creatures from doubling the pressure on a small party. It is a shared budget: two creatures with 3 deeds each, facing 3 PCs, spend 3 deeds between them that round, not 3 each.

> **Example**: A creature with 3 deeds faces a party of 2 PCs. It can spend **2 deeds per round** until more combatants enter the fray.

### One Deed Per Turn

At most one legendary deed resolves per turn, counting every legendary creature in the encounter. If a creature uses a **Legendary Deed: Act** at the end of one creature's turn, no legendary deed of any kind resolves again until after a different creature's turn ends.

Where two creatures would spend a deed in the same turn, the one with the higher tier bonus or challenge rating goes first. On a further tie the Game Director chooses, prioritizing player deeds over monster deeds where possible; the creature that lost the tie may spend its deed at the next turn instead.

This limit counts deeds, not their cost. A single _act_ or _stratagem_ may cost several deeds out of the pool and still be one deed for the purpose of this limit.

---

## Deed Types

Legendary Deeds are organized into four categories, each representing a different facet of the creature's legendary nature.

### Legendary Deed: Act

**Timing**: At the end of another creature's turn.

Acts represent quick, decisive actions the legendary creature can take outside its normal turn. These often mirror or enhance the creature's regular action economy—movement, attacks, or utility abilities.

- Listed with deed costs (e.g., "Costs 1 Deed", "Costs 2 Deeds")
- Can only be used once at a time
- Represent the creature's reflexive combat prowess

> **Example**: "**Lurch** (Costs 2 Deeds). The creature moves up to [= 6 stride =]."

### Legendary Deed: Resist

**Timing**: When the creature fails a saving throw, or at the end of another creature's turn to end all conditions on themselves.

This deed serves to create meaningful choices —does the creature save its deeds for offensive acts, or preserve them to shrug off debilitating effects?
These deeds, unlike others, are limited in usage by a set number of times per rest period (conventionally short), as specified in the creature's stat block.
These deeds conventionally do not exceed 3 uses per rest, to maintain balance and encourage strategic play.

- Typically costs 1 deed.
- Creature can choose to succeed on a failed saving throw
- Some creatures may have enhanced versions that reflect damage or apply conditions

> **Example**: When the creature fails a saving throw, it can choose to succeed instead."

### Legendary Deed: Stratagem

**Timing**: Declared at the end of the creature's turn; resolves at the start of its next turn.

Stratagems are powerful, telegraphed abilities that give players a window to react. They represent the creature's tactical acumen and battlefield control, requiring forethought instead of reflexive action.

- **Declaration**: The creature announces the stratagem at the end of its turn
- **Resolution**: The effect occurs at the start of the creature's next turn
- **Recharge**: Many stratagems recharge only after combat ends
- **Counterplay**: Players have a full round to reposition, use countermeasures, or prepare defenses

> **Example**: "**Consuming Maw** (Stratagem). Declared at end of turn. At the start of next turn, creatures in a [= 12 stride;ADJ =] cone must succeed on a DC 20 Strength save or be swallowed."

### Legendary Deed: Lair

**Timing**: At the start of a round, disregarding initiative.

Lair deeds represent the creature's connection to its environment—the very terrain rising to defend its master. These trigger at a predictable point in combat while still maintaining environmental pressure.

- Trigger at the start of the round, regardless of initiative order
- Cannot use the same lair deed two rounds in a row
- Represent environmental hazards, summoned reinforcements, or terrain manipulation
- Only function while the creature is within its lair

> **Example**: "At the start of the round, the lair floods with poisonous gas. All creatures must succeed on a DC 18 Constitution save or take [% 4d6 poison %]."

### Legendary Deed: Phase

**Timing**: When the creature enters a new band of [# kw:Thresholds and Bands #].

Bands run **Whole**, **Wounded**, **Bloodied**, **Doomed**, **Slain**. **Whole** is where a creature starts, so nothing triggers on entering it, and **Slain** is death, so a phase deed keyed to it resolves as the creature drops rather than after. Phase deeds are therefore written for Wounded, Bloodied, and Doomed, with Slain reserved for a death throe.

Phase deeds always cost one legendary deed, and must be used the moment the creature enters the band.
If the creature is out of legendary deeds when it enters the band, the phase deed activates at the start of its next turn, when it regains them.

Phase deeds represent the creature's transformation as it nears defeat, unlocking new abilities or altering existing ones. These phases often reflect narrative beats in the encounter, signaling a shift in tactics or power level.

> **Example**: "Bloodied (50% HP): the creature sprouts wings and gains a flying speed of [= 12 stride =]."
